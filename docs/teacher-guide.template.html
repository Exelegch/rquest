1. What R Quest is

R Quest teaches children to write real code in R, the language scientists and data analysts use to count, compare and draw charts. Children type short programs, see the answer straight away, and earn stars and badges. Everything runs in the web browser: nothing to install, nothing to pay.

Why R for children?
  • One line of R already does something visible: barplot(c(3, 5, 2)) draws a chart.
  • It builds data skills: counting, averages, sorting and charts.
  • It connects to school maths and to real life, like market prices, savings and the weather.
What children practise
  • Reading and following instructions carefully
  • Predicting what code will do, then checking
  • Finding and fixing mistakes ("debugging") calmly
  • Explaining their thinking to someone else

Faith and language

R Quest is gently Christian: the mascot is a dove, a Bible verse greets the children each day, and some activities use Bible stories (for example, the twelve baskets in John 6). Everything can be switched between English and Yorùbá with one button.

2. How a lesson works

Every lesson has the same three steps. Children cannot skip ahead, but they can always go back.

1 · Try itRun the example code and see what happens. Then change something (a number, a word, a colour) and run it again.
2 · PredictA short "What will R print?" question. Children must think before they press a button. Right first time earns 2 bonus stars.
3 · Your turnA small mission that uses the new idea in a new way. R checks it. A hint is there if they are stuck.
Tip: the Predict step is the best moment to talk. Ask "Why do you think that?" before the child answers. Wrong guesses are useful: the explanation appears straight away.

When a child is stuck

  1. Read R's message together. R Quest adds a plain-English (or Yorùbá) explanation under every error.
  2. Look for the usual suspects: a missing bracket ), a missing quote mark ", a missing comma, or a capital letter where R wants a small one.
  3. Change one small thing at a time, then run again.
  4. Press 💡 Hint. Using hints is fine. Ask the child to explain the hint back to you.

3. Getting started

What you need

  • Any up-to-date browser (Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari) on a computer, tablet or phone.
  • Internet for the first visit. R downloads once (about 20 MB) and is then kept on the device.
  • No accounts. Each child types a first name or nickname.

In a class or group

  • Two children per device works well: one types, one reads the task, then swap.
  • On shared devices, use "Edit" on the My stuff page to change the name, or use different browsers.
  • Parents can make an optional family account to keep progress on several devices.

Settings that help

On My stuff → Settings: bigger text, high contrast, calm mode (less movement), sounds on or off, and language. The 🔈 button reads tasks aloud on devices that support it.

5. A 45-minute session

TimeWhat to do
5 min · warm-upAn "unplugged" game with no devices (ideas below). Link it to today's idea.
5 min · showOn one screen, run today's first lesson together. Ask "What do you think will happen if we change this number?"
25 min · codePairs work through the next lessons on the path. Fast pairs try the unit's bug fix, challenges or a game.

3 min · closeOne sentence each: "Today I learned…". Optional prayer of thanks for the day's verse.

8. Safety and privacy

  • No adverts, no tracking cookies, and data is never sold or shared.
  • Without an account, progress stays on the device. Children never need an email address.
  • R Quest counts anonymous usage (which lessons are finished or hard) with no names. Families can switch this off on the Parents page.
  • Families can delete all their data themselves on the Parents page under Your data.
  • Please remind children not to type full names, addresses or phone numbers into the 💬 feedback box.

The full privacy notice, in English and Yorùbá, is at exelegch.github.io/rquest/privacy.html.

9. Questions

Is it really free? Yes, and it will stay free.

Does it work without the internet? The app opens offline after the first visit, but R itself needs a connection to start. If the connection drops, R starts again by itself when you are back online.

Can I print and adapt the lessons? Yes, for non-commercial teaching. The lessons are under the Creative Commons BY-NC-SA 4.0 licence: share and adapt them for free, name R Quest, and share your changes the same way.

Found a mistake, or have an idea? Use the 💬 button in R Quest, or open an issue at github.com/Exelegch/rquest. Corrections to the Yorùbá are especially welcome.
